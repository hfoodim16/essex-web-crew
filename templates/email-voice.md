# Outreach Voice Guide (email **and** call script)

The outreach is a **draft for Harry to review and send/say himself**. The team never
sends an email or makes a call. A prospect gets EITHER `outreach-email.md` (a real email
address was found) OR `outreach-call.md` (none found — a spoken phone script instead).
The voice below governs both; the call-script section at the end covers what changes.

## Who Harry is (for tone)

A young web designer who builds fast, modern, low-maintenance sites for local businesses
— trades most often, but equally accountants, law offices, auto shops, dentists, vets,
funeral homes: anyone whose site should be a brochure, not an app. Real portfolio piece:
**Cecere Brothers Landscaping**. He's not a faceless agency — the pitch should feel like
a real person who looked at their business and built something on spec.

**Match the register to the reader.** A landscaper reading on a phone between jobs and a
CPA at a desk want the same honesty but not the same tone — keep it plain either way, but
don't write to a professional office like it's a job site.

## Principles

1. **Short.** 120–160 words. Assume it's read on a phone, between other things.
2. **Lead with a specific, real observation** about THEIR business — a service they're
   known for, their town, or exactly what's weak about their current web presence.
   Never open with "I hope this email finds you well."
3. **Show, don't claim.** The hook is that Harry already built them a mockup — with real
   photography of work like theirs and their own logo already in the header. Link it.
4. **Kill the maintenance objection early** — it's a static site, basically nothing to
   maintain, cheap to keep online.
5. **One soft CTA.** "Worth a quick look?" / "Happy to walk you through it." No pressure,
   no fake deadline, no "limited spots."
6. **Proof, lightly.** One line referencing the Cecere Brothers site as an example of
   his work. No exaggeration.
7. **Accurate.** Only facts from the dossier. No invented compliments or stats.

## Structure

```
Subject: <specific — e.g. "A new website for [Business], built and ready to see">

Hi [owner name],

[Specific observation about their business or current site — 1–2 sentences.]

[What Harry did: built a full mockup homepage for them, on spec, no obligation. Link.]

[One line on how low-maintenance/cheap it is to run, killing that objection.]

[Light proof: recently built the site for Cecere Brothers Landscaping.]

[Soft CTA — one question.]

[Harry's name / phone / link]
```

## Call script (when no email was found)

Same voice, spoken. What changes:

- **Lines Harry can actually say** — short spoken sentences, not paragraphs. He should be
  able to read a line without sounding like he's reading.
- **Open with the specific observation**, same as the email: who you are, that you build
  sites for local businesses, and the one real thing you noticed about theirs.
- **The soft ask is to send the link**, not to sell on the call: "can I text or email you
  the link so you can look when you've got a minute?"
- **Prepared responses for the four things they'll say** — they're busy / what does it
  cost / we don't need one / yes, send it. Each gets a short, non-pushy reply.
- **Never**: reading the email aloud, a script that assumes they'll say yes, or pressure.

## One-pager (attach / call notes)

A few bullets: what's weak now, what the mockup fixes, the 3–5 page structure, the
"build once, barely touch it" maintenance story, and the Cecere reference. Plain and
skimmable.

## Never

- No hard sell, fake urgency, or "act now."
- No claims not supported by the dossier.
- No send action — leave it as a draft with `[placeholders]` where Harry personalizes.
