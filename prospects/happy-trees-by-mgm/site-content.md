**Emerald Ash Borer educational article (full):**
- Header: "Emerald Ash Borer Symptoms"
- "emerald ash borer is to blame for the death of millions of ash trees in 27
  states—becoming a particularly prevalent concern in the state of New Jersey."
- "Native to Asia, emerald ash borer is an insect pest that infects and kills ash
  trees." First discovered in the US June 2002, likely introduced ~10 years earlier.
- Description: "about a half-inch long and an 1/8-inch-wide, with a metallic green body
  and copper red underbelly. The larvae…are white or cream colored with an inch-long
  body made of ten bell-shaped abdominal segments."
- "While white and black ash are particularly susceptible to emerald ash borer, all ash
  trees are at risk."
- Lifecycle: adults feed on leaf margins; female lays eggs under bark flaps on the
  trunk; larvae tunnel into bark, feeding on the cambium ("the layer of the tree that
  transports nutrients and water"); adults surface May/early June leaving a 3–4mm
  D-shaped exit hole; adults feed on ash leaves until August; full lifecycle one year.
- "trees will only live about three to four years after an infestation."
- **Signs & symptoms:** woodpecker damage/"flecking" and heightened activity · crown
  dieback (dead branches, leaf loss, discoloration, thinning at the top) · vertical bark
  splits (callus tissue around larval galleries) · D-shaped exit holes (higher canopy
  first, lower trunk as infestation progresses) · epicormic branching (sprouts below the
  feeding larvae, often at the trunk base) · S-shaped larval galleries under the bark
  ("full of frass and sawdust").
- **Ash identification:** branches/buds directly opposite (not staggered) · compound
  leaves with 5–11 leaflets · tight diamond-ridged bark on mature trees, smooth on young
  · seeds are "dry, oar-shaped samaras, usually in clusters…hang on the tree until late
  fall or early winter."
- **Management:** identify and monitor ash trees · use traps · report sightings · ash
  trees CAN be treated to prevent infestation · "Contact Happy Trees today for
  assistance in your ash tree protection."
