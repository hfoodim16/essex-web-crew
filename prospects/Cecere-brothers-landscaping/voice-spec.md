# Voice spec — Cecere Brothers Landscaping

**Rewritten 2026-08-12 from `client-answers.md`** (questionnaire received the same day).
This replaces the thin-answers fallback version written 2026-07-29, which was built with no
client answers on file. Where the two disagree, this one governs.

**Register:** Professional trade — an established local owner talking to a homeowner who
expects high-end work. Q15 words, verbatim: **"Professional, local company with a local
owner, high end."** Banlist Tier 1 still applies.

**Say-aloud persona:** Would **Michael Cecere** — engineering coursework, finance degree,
forty years in the trade, owns the company, answers the phone himself — say this to a
homeowner in Essex Fells? He is not a guy in a truck cracking jokes, and he is not an agency
brochure. He is the licensed owner explaining what his crew will do.

**Note the shift.** The previous spec's persona was "one of the Cecere brothers in your
driveway," which pulled the copy casual. Q15 asks for *high end* alongside *local*. The
target is plain and precise, not folksy — plainness carries the credibility, and the facts
(names, years, licenses, in-house crews) carry the high end. Never reach for luxury
vocabulary to get there.

contractions off** in body copy where his own prose is the source. Short contractions are
acceptable in CTAs and nav.

## Watch list (on top of banlist Tier 1 and Tier 1B)

- `grounds` — estate word; a Caldwell homeowner has a yard. **Target: zero.**
- `tend` / `tending` — same drift ("What we tend to").
- `design-led` — agency vocabulary.
- **Luxury-reach vocabulary** — new, and the risk Q15's "high end" introduces: `bespoke`,
  `curated`, `elevated`, `transform your outdoor living experience`, `stunning`, `oasis`,
  `sanctuary`, `dream yard`. Michael's version of high end is a license number and a crew
  lead who has been there since 1993. Use his.

## Cutesy is the failure mode this site keeps reverting to

Two rounds of copy on this site drifted whimsical, not purple — and whimsy passes every
mechanical check because it's plain and conversational. Watch specifically for:

- **Plants and weather with feelings** — "won't need babying", "the lawn woken up".
- **The crew given mystic perception** — "we read the sun".
- **Landscaping puns** — "Rooted in West Essex", "local roots".
- **Winks** — "Three steps, no mystery", "a lawn that looks handled, because it is".
- **Jokes about the work** — "past the point of no return", "a mess and a deadline".

The owner's standard, in his words: *it's a landscaper, not a dessert.* If a line makes the
reader notice the writing, it's wrong for this site. Q15's "professional" makes this
sharper, not softer.
