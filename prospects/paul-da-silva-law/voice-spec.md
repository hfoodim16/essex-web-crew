## Bar-advertising rules (outrank every other rule here)

These are legal-exposure constraints, not tone preferences:
- **No promised or implied outcomes.** Never "we win," "we'll get it dismissed," "charges
  dropped," win rates, success stats, or case results. The old site's own stated *goal* for
  traffic matters ("aim to reduce or dismiss charges and minimize license points") may be
  carried **as a goal** ("the goal is to…"), never as a promise.
- **No invented credentials.** No bar admissions (none stated anywhere — Confirm list), no
  "top-rated," no "award-winning," no super-lawyer badges, no invented memberships.
- **No fabricated FAQ or legal advice.** No "How much does a DWI cost?" content of any kind.
- **No invented free consultation**, fee, retainer, or payment-plan language.
- **No fabricated reviews.** Only the 3 real Lawyer.com quotes in the dossier, verbatim.
- A standard attorney-advertising / no-attorney-client-relationship footer line is
  appropriate but must be visibly flagged in the mockup as `[Standard disclaimer — for
  Paul's review]`, never asserted as his own approved language.

## How it's written

Full sentences, contractions on ("it's," "we'll," "doesn't"), modest formality allowed in the
bio (Tier 2 permits "we" / "the office"). Digits for numbers ("24 years," "2002," "4 years").
Second person for the reader's situation ("your case," "your closing"); "we/I" only for what
the office actually does. Short paragraphs. Nothing breathless — the reader may be frightened;
the copy's job is to be the calmest thing they've read today.
