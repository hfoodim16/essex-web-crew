Italian-American clients; he also welcomes Spanish-speaking clients. He is **Vice President of

His reviews describe a specific, unusual, and very sellable character: reachable by text or
phone at any hour, plain-spoken to the point of refusing legal jargon, and repeatedly
**declining or shrinking his own fee** when a matter didn't warrant it. Two separate reviewers
say he talked them out of paying him.

**Currently active.** Avvo shows license status **Active** with **"No misconduct found"**; his
Yelp listing was **updated May 2026**; his U.N.I.C.O. role runs "2011–Present". No sign of
retirement, merger, relocation, name change, or a second attorney joining.

## Art-direction hints (for the Planner — not decisions)

The material points somewhere specific and it is **not** the default lawyer look (navy suit,
columns, gavel, stock handshake). His whole differentiator is **the opposite of intimidating**:
plain English, no jargon, never pushy, weekend appointments, calm when things go sideways,
Italian at the kitchen table.

- Register: **warm, personal, local, unpretentious** — a neighborhood professional's office, not
  a downtown firm. Anti-corporate is the point.
- Avoid the category clichés entirely: no gavels, no scales of justice, no marble columns, no
  stock handshakes, no dark-navy authority palette.
- **Typography carries this site**, because there is no logo and no photography. The wordmark is
  the brand. Check `design-memory.md` before choosing — and note the banned set includes
  Fraunces, Instrument Serif, Geist, Plus Jakarta Sans, Space Grotesk on top of the generic four.
- His tagline **"No client too small. No problem too big."** is a natural hero — a two-part
  line that wants a typographic treatment.
- One genuine constraint: **thin imagery.** No logo, no headshot, no job photos. The design must
  work on type, layout and color, with the two `GENERATE` slots doing heavy lifting. If he
  supplies a headshot, that changes the plan — flag it.
