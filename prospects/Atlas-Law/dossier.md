## Ethics note for the Planner (input, not legal advice; confirm against current official text before launch)

**New York changed its advertising rules effective June 1, 2026.** Older checklists are out of date.
- **NY "Attorney Advertising" label: no longer required.** Former Rule 7.1(f) was eliminated. Source: NYSBA article on the amendments (draft 8/19/2026): "Rule 7.1(f) required that advertisements … were required to be labeled 'Attorney Advertising'. That requirement has been eliminated."
- **NY contact information: name + phone OR physical address.** New Rule 7.1(d): "Any communication made under this Rule must include the name and contact information of at least one lawyer or law firm responsible for its content." Comment [10]: "Contact information should always include a telephone number or a physical office address…" Former 7.1(h) (principal office address) was deleted. Source: NYSBA Ethics Opinion 1301 (9/2/2026) ¶¶11–14. → His no-address choice (Q26) is fine **as long as the site names the lawyer and shows the phone number.** Old Rule 1.0(a) "advertisement" definition was deleted too, so Rule 7.1 now covers *all* communications about his services.
- **NY prior-results disclaimer: no longer mandatory**, but the NYSBA article suggests lawyers "may want to consider using" "Prior results do not guarantee a similar outcome." Only relevant if the site describes outcomes. It currently has none.
- **"Specialist" / "certified":** NY Rule 7.1(c) (formerly 7.4): no claim of *certified* specialist unless certified by an approved body named in the communication. Uncertified "concentrates in"/"specializes in" is allowed if not misleading (Comment [7]). **NJ RPC 7.4(a) is stricter:** a lawyer "may not … state or imply that the lawyer has been recognized or certified as a specialist" except as (b)–(d) allow. Since the site must satisfy both states, **avoid "specialist/specializes" entirely**; "practice areas" / "I handle" is safe.
- **No comparisons (NJ RPC 7.1(a)(3)) and nothing misleading (NJ RPC 7.1(a), NY 7.1(a)).** Q6's private reasoning ("outside counsel that will inevitably be far more expensive and possibly less skilled/experienced") **must not go on the site** as a comparison. The in-house-background point (Q3) can be made as a fact about him, not as a claim about other lawyers. No "best/top/leading".
- **Firm name:** "ATLAS LAW" uses his surname. NJ RPC 7.5 (amended Sept 2020) permits names that aren't misleading, comparative, or results-implying. NY retains its own Rule 7.5 (amended June 2020). A surname-based name raises no obvious issue.
- **Jurisdiction line:** he's admitted in both NJ and NY, so state "Admitted in New Jersey and New York". It's accurate and helps with NJ RPC 7.5's jurisdictional-limitations concept. "Can service clients anywhere" (Q4) should be worded so it doesn't imply admission elsewhere (e.g., "advises businesses in NJ, NY and beyond on matters within his admissions" is the Planner's to phrase).
- Standard NJ line for any award/rating (none here): "No aspect of this advertisement has been approved by the Supreme Court of New Jersey." Not needed unless a rating is shown.
- Outside site scope, for Harry only: NY Judiciary Law §470 requires non-resident NY attorneys to keep a NY office (Opn. 1301 ¶7 discusses it). Matt will know his own situation. Don't put this on the site.
